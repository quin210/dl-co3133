1. Model definitions

All five models receive normalized images [B, 1, 28, 28] and return ten raw logits [B, 10]. Cross-entropy receives the logits directly. Softmax gives class probabilities only after prediction. All models train for 16 epochs, and validation macro-F1 selects best.pt. There is no dropout, weight decay or augmentation. Gradient clipping limits the gradient norm to 5.

Model definitions. Parameter counts come from the saved seed-42 runs.
ModelInput to the modelLayersParameters
LinearFlattened image, 784 valuesLinear(784, 10). No hidden layer and no nonlinear activation. This is the baseline.7,850
MLPFlattened image, 784 values784 → 256 → 10, ReLU after the hidden layer. Dense layers do not encode spatial neighborhoods.203,530
CNNImage, 1 × 28 × 28Two padded 3 × 3 convolutions (16 and 32 channels), each with ReLU and 2 × 2 max-pooling: 16 × 14 × 14 → 32 × 7 × 7. Then 1568 → 64 → 10.105,866
LSTM28 rows of 28 pixels, top to bottomOne unidirectional LSTM layer, hidden size 64. The final hidden state [B, 64] goes to Linear(64, 10).24,714
Transformer49 patches of 4 × 4 pixelsPatch projection 16 → 32 plus learned positions. Two encoder layers, four heads of eight features, feedforward 32 → 64 → 32. Mean of the 49 tokens, then Linear(32, 10).19,530

The full definitions, shapes and code links are on the Methods page.

10. Results discussion

The 256-unit MLP adds nonlinear features to the flattened 784-pixel input. Over five seeds, it increases the mean test macro-F1 from 84.15% to 88.77% over Linear. Both models lose the explicit two-dimensional neighborhood structure, but the MLP can combine pixels nonlinearly. The two models also have different parameter counts and computation. Thus, this comparison does not prove that nonlinearity alone causes the gain.

CNN is the validation-selected model in the predefined primary run (seed 42). Its local 3 × 3 filters and pooling use the spatial structure of the image. CNN gets 91.27 ± 0.27% mean test macro-F1. It is 2.50 macro-F1 points better than MLP (better in all five seeds) with approximately half the parameters. But its measured batched CPU inference time is approximately 13 times the MLP time per image. CNN is the better choice for accuracy here. MLP gives a useful trade-off for inference cost. On average, the sequential fit takes approximately 105 s for CNN and 8 s for MLP, with the same 16-epoch schedule. Examine the inference cost and the training cost separately.

LSTM reads the 28 image rows in sequence and keeps a summary in its final hidden state. It gets 88.10% mean test macro-F1 with 24,714 parameters. This is 0.66 points below MLP in each seed, and LSTM inference is slower than MLP inference. Transformer uses attention over 49 patches of 4 × 4 pixels with learned positions. It gets 87.06% with 19,530 parameters, and it selects its final epoch for seed 42. These compact sequence models have different spatial assumptions and optimization needs. Their current scores do not show that sequence models cannot learn images. They also do not show that more training will improve them.

The main limitation that stays is the ambiguity between upper-body garments. Shirt is still the weakest CNN class. CNN still confuses Shirt and T-shirt/top in both directions (104 and 94 test images, seed 42). The footwear errors in Section 8 also show that high softmax confidence does not guarantee a correct label. This result agrees with the overlapping silhouettes in the EDA. But visual similarity is a description, and the classification errors are a measurement. We keep the two separate.

As a next step, use the training and validation data to test a longer Transformer schedule. Also test small image shifts or weight decay for the nonlinear models. These are proposed experiments, not measured improvements. Do not select their settings from the test results. The current comparison uses one split, five training seeds and a fixed 16-epoch schedule, with no tuning for each architecture. Section 11 examines one alternative explanation: the parameter count.

11. Ablation: matched parameter budget (~25k)

Question
Does CNN win because of its spatial design (convolutions), or because it has more parameters than LSTM and Transformer?
Hypothesis
When each model has approximately 25k parameters, CNN still has the highest macro-F1.
Changed
Model size only. MLP hidden layer 31 units (was 256). CNN dense layer 13 units (was 64) with the same convolutions. Transformer width 36 (was 32) and feedforward size 72 (was 64). The existing LSTM runs (24,714 parameters) are used again.
Fixed
Split, seeds 42–46, preprocessing, optimizer, learning rate, batch size, 16 epochs, checkpoint rule and hardware.
